The collector craft

A cutter cuts and a COLLECTOR carries. Today one civil hull does both: it flies to a rock, bores a shaft, and tows its cubes home in a line behind it. This is the other shape, with the hauling split off onto small craft that pick a cube up in two arms and bring it back.

This is a mockup, and nothing here is in the game yet.

The rule this project keeps is that a new hull or a new mechanic is rendered, linked and approved before it is built, because the move order shipped four defects the first time it was written straight into the game.

What it is

A hull too small to be on the ladder

Fighter sized, and that is the point

The smallest thing on the fleet's ladder is a corvette at half a frigate, and a corvette is still a warship. A collector is the size of a fighter: a cab, a tank, a drive and two arms, with no gun, no reactor worth the name and no business anywhere a shot is going.

So it is not a rung and it is not on the ladder. It is a CRAFT, the way a fighter is: built in a hangar slot rather than a berth, and replaced rather than repaired.

Two arms, and they are the whole silhouette

Three segments a side, folded back along the hull when empty and reaching forward to close on a cube. A cube is 0.36 of a cutter's radius, so the arms have to span roughly the craft's own width: what you are looking at when one is loaded is mostly cargo.

Folded and reaching are two poses of one rig, eased between, so a collector on its way out and a collector on its way home are different shapes at a glance.

One cube at a time

No hold, no capacity number, no stacking. A collector carries the one thing in its arms and that is the entire cargo model, which is what keeps it readable from across the field: count the craft with something in them and you have counted the cargo in flight.

What it costs

Three things this adds, named so they can be argued with

A pile is a new thing on the field

A cube that waits at the rock is a cube nothing owns. It has to sit still, it has to be visible from the range a player picks targets at, and something has to decide which collector goes for which one. Nearest free cube to the nearest idle collector is the whole rule, and it is the rule to check first.

A craft is not a hull

Fighters already exist as entities with no voxel model and no damage grid, and a collector is the same kind of thing. That keeps it cheap and it means a collector cannot be chewed, wounded or wrecked: it is alive or it is gone, exactly as a fighter is.

The arms are the only new geometry

Everything else on the craft is a box. The arms are two rigs of three segments, posed from one number between nought and one, and that number is the only thing the sim has to publish about them.

Four decisions that are not mine

1. Does the cutter still carry at all? The simplest rule is no: it cuts, it piles, it never goes home. That makes a system with no collectors in it produce nothing, which may be the right pressure or may be a trap.

2. Where do collectors come from? A hangar slot on the ship, like fighters, launched whole and replaced on a timer. Or a yard row you pay for one at a time.

3. How many? Two per cutter roughly keeps up with one shaft at the cut rate above. A ceiling is what stops the answer being "all of them".

4. Do they get shot? A collector in the open with a cube in its arms is the most attackable thing in the fleet, which is either the point or a chore.
