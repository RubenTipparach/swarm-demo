What each part is for

Nothing explains itself

Every label is a noun or a verb and no control carries a sentence. The reinforcements button and its paragraph of bindings go back to the sandbox, which is where a button that exists to be tested belongs.

The rail is how you find a ship

One button per capital, in the order they were built, with a reactor sliver on each. Clicking one selects it and eases the camera onto it; the number keys are the same action asked for twice, exactly as R and the old button were.

Support hulls are on it too, dimmer, because a miner you cannot find is a miner you cannot redirect.

Orders are a mode, not a drag

The order row changes with what is selected: a capital gets move, attack and hold, a wing gets patrol, escort and recall. Arming one puts OrderMode where the existing nav disc already reads it, so a left click is a commit and never a selection.

The yard is a drawer

Shut by default, one row of state on its tab: what is building and how many berths are left. A card the bank cannot afford is dim rather than absent, and the reason is on the card, because "no berth" and "not enough" are two different things to do next.

Research buys the right to buy

Data unlocks a role or the next rung of your own navy, and the ladder is read off the fleet manifest rather than typed. A locked node shows what it needs first, so the tree is a plan rather than a wall.

Upkeep is per cell

A bar per hull against its reactor, the open cells counted beside it, and one button that welds them at a material a cell. Tender crews take the price down and the banner says by how much, since a discount nobody sees is a discount nobody plans around.

Mockup only. Numbers on it are the ones already in the code (an ore cube lands 120 materials, a crystal cube 50 volatiles, a data cube 30, an escort is two ore cubes, welding is one material a cell, three tender crews cap the discount at half). Nothing here is wired to the game yet: approve it and it becomes the HUD.
