Baked off the voxel model, and drawn beside it

Every picture of a SHIP in that HUD is generated rather than drawn. One function meshes a model with the same greedy pass the battlefield uses, renders it to an offscreen target under an orthographic camera, reads the pixels back and runs one rim pass over the alpha. One bake per hull, shown at five sizes, so nothing can drift between the list and the panel. On the Rust side this is the ship_export example writing PNGs beside the hulls, off greedy_mesh and the purpose byte the export already carries.

And the six class marks, which are DRAWN

A class is not a ship, so no ship can stand for one: a carrier's picture on the capital button says that navy's carrier rather than capital. These are authored line art in the same plan view the schematics are baked in, blocked out the way the lattice is, carrying no faction paint and no livery, and they are currentColor so a button tints them with its own state. Six marks serve every navy in the game and every hull added to one.

The frames are nine sliced

Every panel and well is one 48 pixel texture with a 16 pixel slice, so a corner bracket is the same size on the resource strip and on the build menu and nothing stretches that should not. These are baked at boot on a 2D canvas. In Bevy they are an ImageNode with ImageScaleMode::Sliced and the same four numbers; the checker behind them is the transparency, since a HUD that was opaque would be a HUD that took the field away.

What each decision is

One layer at a fixed design size

The HUD is authored at 1600 by 900 and scaled to whatever the viewport is, which is how a game does it and why every hairline stays a hairline. Nothing reflows, so a screenshot at one size is a screenshot at all of them.

A schematic is a PLAN, in two materials

Straight down, with the length across the frame, because a ship seen from above is its layout and the layout is what the panel is asking a player to read. It is also the one view every hull in a fleet can be held against another in.

Hull quads fill dark and outline cyan; any cell with a purpose (propulsion, gun, ordnance, command) fills and outlines orange. That is the export's own byte, so a ship says what it is made of without anybody labelling it, and a hull shot to pieces will read as one.

A class is DRAWN, a ship is BAKED

One rule, and it holds everywhere. A ship is shown by its own schematic, in the build row, the queue slot, the roster line, the producer and the unit readout, so a hull looks the same in all five. A class is shown by a drawn mark, because a class is every navy's version of a thing and a picture of one navy's carrier is not that.

The greedy mesher is what draws the panel lines

Every quad's own border is a line segment, so a big plate is one rectangle and a greebled stern is dozens. The schematic gets its detail from the mesher rather than from an artist, which is the only way twenty seven hulls get one.

A class is told apart by its SHIPS

Six drawn marks, in the same plan view the schematics are baked in and blocked out the way the lattice is: a swept dart, a hull with two pods, a hull with a bow gun, a broad hull with three drives, a hauler with clamps, and a mount with no drives at all. They carry no faction paint, so they serve every navy in the game and every hull added to one.

The roster is half the size it was and carries no words at all: a line is a schematic and a quantity, and a rule between classes does the grouping a heading used to. The cap came off with the label, because what a class is ALLOWED is a number nobody reads in a fight and what it HAS is the only thing that moves. It is read off the fleet rather than typed beside it, so a class added tomorrow is on it tomorrow, and a line of it PICKS: clicking one selects that ship.

The row under Build is the MODULES

Not the build menu a second time. It is what bolts ON to the hull that is selected: production, hangar, armour, drives, sensors, refinery. Gold is installed, cyan is on the subsystem queue, and dim is a fit this class cannot take or a hull with no slot left. Clicking an available one spends resource units and puts it on that queue, which is what the Subsystem Queue line above the yard was always for.

It sits under Build, Research and Launch because those three decide what the YARD does and this decides what one SHIP is. The header counts the slots, because a module row with no count is six buttons whose refusal has no reason on screen.

Two resources, and the second one is a countdown

Ore is a number. Crystal is not: it is fuel against what a JUMP costs, so it is a bar with the need written over it. The need is the drive plus every other hull standing in the field, which is the Long Retreat's own rule, so laying down an escort visibly sets the jump back and the bar answers "can I leave yet" without a player doing arithmetic. It goes green when it can.

Queueing is real

A row costs resource units, lands in the ship queue with a bar, and when it finishes the hull is spawned into the field and the class strip and the roster both count it. A row you cannot afford is dim with its price in red, and the producer's production slots are the cap. The producer is a hull standing in the field rather than a class, so the slots it reports are that ship's own.

Nothing refuses a purchase but the bank. What a yard cannot do is WORK on two hulls of a class at once, which is a fact about the slip rather than about the queue: the earliest entry of each class advances, up to the producer's production slots, and the rest stand in line with a dashed edge. So you spend every unit the moment you have it, the order you spent it in is the order it arrives, and the row counts what you have stacked.

Selection is an OUTLINE, drawn on the ship

Brackets, rings and lines are all a shape drawn NEAR a hull, sized off a bounding box that on a long ship is mostly empty: a frigate seen end on wore a mark three times its own width. The selected hull is outlined instead, which is the one mark that is always exactly the ship.

The same geometry a second time, back faces only, swollen by a constant world thickness per axis so the shell is even rather than heavier at the bow, depth tested and drawn after the hull, so the inside fails against the ship's own depth and only the ring outside its silhouette survives. The thickness is set in PIXELS and converted per frame, because a marker nobody can see at the zoom they are actually at is a marker that is not there.

The integrity bar sits ABOVE the hull and on the selected one alone. It stays in screen space because a bar has to be the same number of pixels tall at every range; the outline is the ship's own shape, so it does not.

Placeholder hulls, generated in the page from a voxel lattice on the same material and purpose codes the game uses. The real fleet is twenty three stock hulls and four alien archetypes; the baker reads the hull directory rather than a list, so they arrive without anything here changing.
